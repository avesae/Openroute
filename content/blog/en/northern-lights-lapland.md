The northern lights are the main reason travellers from around the world come to Finnish Lapland. Here's how to boost your chances of seeing them.

## When to go

The season runs from late August to early April, but the best months are **September–October and February–March**: nights are dark and the weather is more often clear. December–January are dark almost all day, but cloudier.

## Where to watch

The further from city lights, the better. Popular spots:

- **Levi and Kittilä** — easy to combine with skiing
- **Saariselkä** — glass igloos and Urho Kekkonen National Park
- **Inari and Utsjoki** — the far north with minimal light pollution

## How to forecast

Watch the cloud forecast and the Kp index. In Lapland, **Kp 2–3** is enough. Apps like *My Aurora Forecast* send alerts when activity is high.

## How to shoot on your phone

1. Use night mode or manual mode with a 3–10 second exposure.
2. Put the phone on a tripod or a steady surface.
3. Turn off the flash and lower the screen brightness.

## What to wear

Thermal underwear, a wool jumper, a down jacket, warm boots and mittens. On our Lapland tours, thermal suits are provided on site.
